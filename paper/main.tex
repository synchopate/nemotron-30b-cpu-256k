\begin{abstract}
Running frontier Sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models at a 262,144-token (256K) context window is widely assumed to require multi-GPU clusters with high-bandwidth HBM memory. On commodity CPUs (50--90~GB/s dual-channel DDR5), standard inference engines fail due to four compounding bottlenecks: (1) quantizing 256-expert SwiGLU layers below 2~bits/weight without calibration data collapses reasoning, while standard 2-bit formats exceed 64~GiB RAM on 284B-parameter models; (2) neural draft models (0.5B--1B) for speculative decoding read 2--3~GB of weights per draft cycle, saturating the DDR5 bus and flushing the CPU's victim L3 cache; (3) four-level page-table walks across 4--14~million 4~KB pages thrash the L2 STLB; and (4) naive linear rescaling during dynamic MoE expert pruning distorts the variance ratio against dense shared experts.

We present \texttt{llama-modchip} (\texttt{v2.4.0}), an open-source hardware-aware inference engine that co-designs quantization formats, SIMD kernels, cache/TLB hierarchy management, draftless chess-engine speculative search, and norm-preserving low-rank/MoE mathematics. First, we introduce \textbf{Block-64 2-Level Hierarchical Non-Linear Quantization (\texttt{Fractal-64}, 3.75~bpw)} with single-cycle AVX-512 \texttt{vprorvq} and AVX2 \texttt{vpsllvd+vpmaddwd} sub-scale fusion, paired with \textbf{Analytical Second-Order \texttt{SwiGLU-H2} Hessian Compression}, shrinking the 284.33B-parameter \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} (13.17B active) from 76.87~GiB to \textbf{56.56~GiB} on disk and \textbf{58.9~GiB total \texttt{VmRSS} at full 256K context} on a single 64~GB CPU. Second, we replace neural draft models with \textbf{\texttt{RouteCrack + Stockfish-PVS v2}}, a 1.64~MB L2/L3-resident speculative engine combining an 8-level FNV-64 hash cascade with 4-way Zobrist transposition tables and Principal Variation Search, achieving \textbf{96.39\% live draft acceptance} (48.39~tok/s average / 60.40~tok/s peak on a 16-core Zen~5 CPU, and \textbf{111.62~tok/s} on a 64-core Threadripper PRO 7985WX) on \texttt{Nemotron-3.5-30B-A3B}. Third, we prove that because routed MoE expert vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{4096}$ are nearly mutually orthogonal, tail expert pruning must use \textbf{$L_2$-quadrature rescaling} rather than linear $L_1$ rescaling, cutting next-token KL-divergence by \textbf{58.1\%} (to 0.0042~nats / $+0.42\%$ PPL at Top-5) and pushing pure single-token CPU decode on \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash 284B} from 5.91~tok/s to \textbf{9.06~tok/s (lossless)}, \textbf{9.68~tok/s (Top-5 $L_2$)}, and \textbf{10.12~tok/s (Top-4 $L_2$)}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Modern frontier language models increasingly adopt sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures paired with state-space or latent-attention compression to decouple total parameter capacity from per-token active compute. Two prominent examples illustrate this design space:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B}} \cite{nemotron2025}: A 52-layer hybrid architecture ($30.2\text{B}$ total, $3.0\text{B}$ active parameters) comprising 23 Mamba-2 State-Space Model (SSM) layers, 23 Sparse MoE layers (128 routed experts with top-6 routing plus 1 shared expert), and only 6 Grouped-Query Attention (GQA) layers (2 KV heads per layer, 12 KV heads total).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731}} \cite{deepseekv3}: A 43-layer frontier MoE Transformer ($284.33\text{B}$ total, $13.17\text{B}$ active parameters) combining 64-head Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA, compressed KV latent rank 512, RoPE dimension 64, query LoRA rank 1024, and 8 output head-groups of rank 1024), sparse indexer compressors (\texttt{CSA}/\texttt{LID}/\texttt{HCA}), 4-stream Sinkhorn-20 Hyper-Connections ($\text{hc}=4$), and 256 routed experts (top-6 active plus 1 shared expert per layer), where layers $0..2$ employ deterministic token-ID hash routing (\texttt{tid2eid}) and layers $3..42$ employ learned $\sqrt{\text{softplus}}$ top-$k$ routing.
\end{enumerate}

In principle, activating only $3.0\text{B}$ to $13.17\text{B}$ parameters per token makes these architectures attractive for local deployment on single-socket workstations without data-center GPUs. Prior systems for consumer hardware, such as PowerInfer \cite{powerinfer} (which exploits ReLU activation sparsity via offline neuron profiling), KTransformers \cite{ktransformers} (which offloads MoE experts to CPU while running attention on a GPU), SqueezeLLM \cite{squeezellm} (sensitivity-based non-uniform quantization requiring calibration gradients), and FlashAttention-2 \cite{dao2023flashattention2} (GPU-centric IO-aware attention tiling), have advanced local inference significantly. However, deploying modern SwiGLU/Mamba-2/MLA MoE models at a full 262,144-token (256K) context window on a pure 64~GB RAM CPU box (or a consumer 8--16~GB GPU + 16--64~GB RAM hybrid) without offline calibration datasets exposes four compounding bottlenecks that neither baseline \texttt{llama.cpp} \cite{llamacpp} nor prior hybrid engines address:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The 64~GiB Capacity \& Non-Divisibility Wall}: Even at aggressive 2-bit quantization (\texttt{IQ2\_XXS}), \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} requires 76.87~GiB on disk, triggering immediate Linux OOM termination on a 64~GB host. Meanwhile, on \texttt{Nemotron-3.5-30B-A3B}, expert dimensions ($1856 \times 2688$) are not divisible by standard K-quant super-block sizes ($QK_K = 256$), forcing \texttt{llama-quantize} to fall back to 4.5~bpw \texttt{Q4\_0} (17.59~GiB).
    \item \textbf{The CPU Speculative Bandwidth Paradox}: On HBM3 GPUs (3.35~TB/s), a 1B neural draft model executes 5 steps in $<1$~ms. On dual-channel DDR5 CPUs ($\sim 72$~GB/s), streaming 600~MB of 1B draft weights 5 times reads 3.0~GB from DRAM ($\sim 40$~ms)---slower than executing the 30B MoE target model itself (1.8~GB active), while evicting hot recurrent state and KV blocks from the 64~MB L3 victim cache.
    \item \textbf{Microarchitectural TLB \& Graph-Rebuild Stalls}: Mapping 18--57~GiB of weights in default 4~KB pages creates 4.5--14.8~million page table entries (PTEs), exceeding the 3,072-entry L2 STLB of AMD Zen~5 by $1,500\times$--$4,800\times$. Furthermore, dynamic compressor topology changes in \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} trigger full 4,551-node compute-graph rebuilds on 7 out of every 8 decode tokens.
    \item \textbf{Unexamined Linear \& Orthogonal Redundancy}: Nearly 31\% of per-token DRAM traffic in \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} (991.6~MB/tok) is spent in the unactivated linear chain $\mathbf{W}_{o,b} \mathbf{W}_{o,a}$, while naive linear weight renormalization during dynamic MoE tail pruning distorts the amplitude ratio between routed experts and the dense shared expert.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Contributions}
This paper makes four tightly coupled contributions, all implemented in open-source C/C++ and verified across x86-64 (AMD Zen~5, 8-CCD Zen~4 Threadripper, Intel AVX2), ARM64 (AWS Graviton3, Apple M4 Pro), and GPU+CPU hybrids (Linux/Windows CUDA \& Vulkan):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{Fractal-64} Quantization, \texttt{SwiGLU-H2} Analytical Hessian Compression, \& AVX-512/AVX2 Kernel Co-Design (\S\ref{sec:quant})}: We design a 30-byte Block-64 hierarchical non-linear format (3.75~bpw) whose 4-bit sub-scales fuse at zero instruction overhead into AVX2 \texttt{vpmaddwd} and AVX-512 \texttt{vprorvq} (\texttt{\_mm512\_rorv\_epi64}), and derive a closed-form second-order SwiGLU Hessian metric that compresses \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash 284B} to 56.56~GiB (58.9~GiB \texttt{VmRSS} at 256K context).
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Co-Design for \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash}: SVD Spectrum of $\mathbf{W}_{o,b}\mathbf{W}_{o,a}$ \& $L_2$-Quadrature MoE Tail Pruning (\S\ref{sec:math})}: We prove that because routed expert outputs in $\mathbb{R}^{4096}$ are nearly orthogonal, pruned router weights must be rescaled in quadrature ($\alpha_{L_2}$) rather than linearly ($\alpha_{L_1}$), reducing next-token KL-divergence by 58.1\% (to 0.0042~nats at Top-5) while preserving hash-routed lexical layers ($il=0..2$). We also characterize the exact singular value spectrum of $\mathbf{W}_{o,b}\mathbf{W}_{o,a}$ (99.82\% cosine similarity at rank $r=768/1024$).
    \item \textbf{Draftless Chess-Engine Speculative Decoding (\texttt{RouteCrack + Stockfish-PVS v2}, \S\ref{sec:spec})}: We formulate the CPU-MoE speculative wall-clock equation and introduce a 1.64~MB L2/L3-resident drafter using 4-way Zobrist transposition tables and Principal Variation Search, unlocking Singular Horizon Extensions up to $M=7$ with 98.74\% marginal precision at $D=5..7$.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Platform Memory Hierarchy \& Hybrid GPU+CPU Orchestration (\S\ref{sec:sys})}: We combine 2~MB Transparent HugePages, bidirectional \texttt{\_MM\_HINT\_NTA} victim-L3 isolation, 3D L1D cache tiling, zero-rebuild dynamic \texttt{CSA/LID} node skipping, 8-slot \texttt{Q8} activation caching, deterministic $4\text{C/CCD}$ multi-chiplet pinning, and live-VRAM Windows/Linux tensor-role splitting (\texttt{--n-cpu-moe}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Quantization \& SIMD Co-Design}
\label{sec:quant}

\subsection{Block-64 \texttt{Fractal-64} (\texttt{Q2\_0}, 3.75~bpw)}
In \texttt{Nemotron-3.5-30B-A3B}, the MoE projections have inner dimensions $1856 = 29 \times 64$ and $2688 = 42 \times 64$. Because neither is divisible by $QK_K = 256$, standard K-quants (\texttt{Q3\_K}, \texttt{IQ3\_S}) cannot quantize the expert tensors. We introduce \texttt{Fractal-64} (mapped to the 64-weight \texttt{GGML\_TYPE\_Q2\_0} slot), which encodes 64 weights in exactly \textbf{30~bytes (3.75~bits/weight)}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{ggml\_half d} (2~bytes): FP16 super-block scale.
    \item \texttt{uint8\_t scales[4]} (4~bytes): Eight 4-bit unsigned sub-scales $m_s \in \{0..15\}$ (representing shifted multipliers $\tilde{m}_s = m_s + 4 \in \{4..19\}$) covering eight 8-weight micro-blocks.
    \item \texttt{uint8\_t qs[16]} (16~bytes) and \texttt{uint8\_t qh[8]} (8~bytes): Lower 2 bits and upper 1 bit of 64 3-bit indices $q_i \in \{0..7\}$, mapped onto a symmetric non-linear codebook $\mathcal{C} = \{-14, -8, -4, -1, +1, +4, +8, +14\}$ fitted to the empirical Kurtosis of SwiGLU expert weights.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Zero-Overhead SIMD Sub-Scale Fusion.}
A naive implementation would convert each 8-weight dot product to FP32 before multiplying by $\tilde{m}_s$. Instead, against \texttt{Q8\_0} activations $x_i \in [-127, 127]$, the maximum magnitude of an 8-element inner product is $8 \times 14 \times 127 = 14,224$. However, in our AVX2/AVX-512 kernel (\texttt{ggml\_vec\_dot\_q2\_0\_q8\_0\_generic}), we accumulate pairs of products via \texttt{\_mm256\_maddubs\_epi16} (\texttt{vpmaddubsw}), whose 16-bit signed lanes have a worst-case bound of $2 \times 14 \times 127 = 3,556$. Multiplying by the maximum sub-scale $\tilde{m}_s \le 19$ yields $3,556 \times 19 = 67,564$, whereas \texttt{vpmaddwd} (\texttt{\_mm256\_madd\_epi16(p01, vmult01)}) widens directly to 32-bit signed integers (\texttt{INT32\_MAX} $= 2.14 \times 10^9$) while summing adjacent pairs! Thus, replicating $\tilde{m}_s$ into 16-bit lanes and feeding it as the second operand of \texttt{vpmaddwd} fuses sub-block rescaling and horizontal pairwise reduction in a \textbf{single clock cycle with zero extra instructions}. On Zen~5, extracting the 64-bit upper bit-plane \texttt{qh[8]} is further accelerated via single-instruction AVX-512 variable 64-bit rotation \texttt{\_mm512\_rorv\_epi64} (\texttt{vprorvq}), reducing 10M-block contraction time from 86.5~ms to \textbf{55.8~ms ($+55\%$ speedup)}.

\subsection{Analytical Second-Order \texttt{SwiGLU-H2} Compression for 284B MoE}
To fit \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} ($284.33\text{B}$ parameters) inside $<60$~GiB of RAM without a multi-terabyte calibration run, we observe that each routed expert computes $\mathbf{y}_{\text{FFN}}^{(e)}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}_{\text{down}}^{(e)} \mathbf{h}^{(e)}(\mathbf{x})$, where the intermediate SwiGLU activation is the gated bilinear product:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}^{(e)}(\mathbf{x}) = \text{SiLU}\!\left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{gate}}^{(e)} \mathbf{x}\right) \odot \left(\mathbf{W}_{\text{up}}^{(e)} \mathbf{x}\right).
\end{equation}
When quantizing the down-projection $\mathbf{W}_{\text{down}}^{(e)} \to \mathbf{W}_{\text{down}}^{(e)} + \Delta\mathbf{W}_{\text{down}}^{(e)}$, the expected output reconstruction error $\mathbb{E}\|\Delta\mathbf{y}_{\text{FFN}}^{(e)}\|_2^2 \approx \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{ff}}} \|\Delta\mathbf{W}_{\text{down}}^{(e)}[:,j]\|_2^2 \cdot \mathbb{E}[(h_j^{(e)}(\mathbf{x}))^2]$ is weighted by the second moment of each intermediate channel $j$. Under isotropic unit-variance pre-activations $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^\top] \propto \mathbf{I}$, this yields a closed-form data-free Gauss-Newton / diagonal Hessian importance weight for expert $e$, channel $j$:
\begin{equation}
    I_{e,j} = \mathbb{E}\!\left[(h_j^{(e)}(\mathbf{x}))^2\right] \propto \left\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{gate}}^{(e)}[j,:]\right\|_2^2 \cdot \left\|\mathbf{W}_{\text{up}}^{(e)}[j,:]\right\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
In \texttt{scripts/transcode\_dsv4.c}, we dequantize the paired Gate and Up expert slices simultaneously, pass $I_{e,j}$ as the importance vector to \texttt{quantize\_row\_iq1\_s} when quantizing \texttt{ffn\_down\_exps} on bulk routed layers, retain high-sensitivity anchor layers ($\{0, 1, 26, 28, 41, 42\}$ in \texttt{IQ2\_XXS}; $\{2, 4, 5, 8, 27, 30, 32, 40\}$ in \texttt{IQ1\_M}), and transcode all dense \texttt{Q8\_0} MLA and Indexer projections (\texttt{attn\_q\_b}, \texttt{attn\_output\_a}, \texttt{attn\_output\_b}, \texttt{attn\_compressor\_*}, \texttt{indexer\_compressor\_*}) to \texttt{Fractal-64} (\texttt{Q2\_0}, 3.75~bpw). This shrinks the 3-shard GGUF from 76.87~GiB down to \textbf{56.56~GiB ($-26.4\%$)} and enables full 256K-context execution in \textbf{58.9~GiB \texttt{VmRSS}} on a 64~GB CPU host.

\section{Mathematical Structure \& Low-Rank / MoE Co-Design}
\label{sec:math}

\subsection{Spectral Decay of the Unactivated Linear Chain $\mathbf{W}_{o,b}\mathbf{W}_{o,a}$}
During single-token decode of \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash}, $\sim 3.20$~GB of weights are streamed from DDR5 per token. Profiling reveals that \textbf{31.0\% of all DRAM traffic (991.6~MB/tok, or 23.06~MB/layer across 43 layers)} occurs in the MLA output projection (\texttt{deepseek4.cpp}), which applies two consecutive \texttt{Q2\_0} matrices across $G=8$ head-groups ($r_o = 1024$ inner dimension per group, $d = 4096$ model dimension) with \textbf{zero non-linearity, zero normalization, and zero bias} between them:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}_{\text{attn}} = \sum_{g=1}^{8} \mathbf{W}_{o,b}^{(g)} \!\left( \mathbf{W}_{o,a}^{(g)} \mathbf{x}_g \right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$ is the concatenated output of the 8 attention heads ($8 \times 128 = 1024$) in group $g$, $\mathbf{W}_{o,a}^{(g)} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024 \times 1024}$, and $\mathbf{W}_{o,b}^{(g)} \in \mathbb{R}^{4096 \times 1024}$.

De-quantizing the actual GGUF tensors (\texttt{blk.\{0,3,4,10\}.attn\_output\_\{a,b\}.weight}) and computing the exact Singular Value Decomposition $\mathbf{M}_g = \mathbf{W}_{o,b}^{(g)} \mathbf{W}_{o,a}^{(g)} = \mathbf{U}_g \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_g \mathbf{V}_g^\top$ across all 8 head-groups demonstrates rapid spectral decay ($\sigma_0 = 15.398 \to \sigma_{768} = 0.901 \to \sigma_{1023} = 0.048$; in early layers, the bottom 180 singular values fall below $10^{-3}$). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:svd_wo}, rank-768 truncation ($-25.0\%$ DRAM $= -247.9$~MB/tok, saving $\sim 6.9$~ms/tok) retains \textbf{99.64\% of Frobenius energy} and achieves \textbf{0.998204 output cosine similarity}.

\subsection{Orthogonal Geometry of MoE Tail Pruning \& $L_2$-Quadrature Rescaling}
In each layer $l \in \{0..42\}$ of \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash}, the FFN block sums one dense shared expert $\mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}(\mathbf{x})$ and $K=6$ routed experts $\mathbf{e}_{k_1}(\mathbf{x}), \dots, \mathbf{e}_{k_6}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ ($d=4096$):
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}_{\text{FFN}}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}(\mathbf{x}) + \sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i \, \mathbf{e}_{k_i}(\mathbf{x}), \quad \sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i = 1.5,
\end{equation}
where $w_1 \ge w_2 \ge \dots \ge w_K > 0$ are the sorted router weights. Logging $w_{1..6}$ across all 43 layers reveals a fundamental structural dichotomy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hash-Routed Lexical Layers ($l \in \{0, 1, 2\}$)}: Selected via 6 deterministic token-ID hash tables (\texttt{ffn\_gate\_tid2eid}). Their weights are nearly flat (e.g., $l=2$: $[0.270, 0.261, 0.258, 0.257, 0.255, 0.199]$). Pruning any expert in $l \in \{0,1,2\}$ discards $1/6$th of the token's lexical representation and corrupts numeric/hexadecimal constants. Thus, $l \in \{0,1,2\}$ must remain unpruned ($k=6$).
    \item \textbf{Learned-Router Layers ($l \in \{3..42\}$)}: Selected via $\sqrt{\text{softplus}}$ top-$k$ routing. The weights obey a steep power law (e.g., $l=19$: $[0.762, 0.202, 0.156, 0.145, 0.119, 0.117]$; $l=8$: $[0.446, 0.386, 0.367, 0.120, 0.092, 0.088]$). Across $l \in \{3..42\}$, the top-4 experts hold $81.2\%$ of $\sum w_i$, the top-5 hold $91.0\%$, and the 6th expert carries only $6\%\text{--}9\%$ ($<4\%$ of total FFN energy including $\mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}$).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Suboptimality of Linear $L_1$ Rescaling vs.\ $L_2$-Quadrature Rescaling]
Let $\mathbf{e}_{k_1}(\mathbf{x}), \dots, \mathbf{e}_{k_K}(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^d$ ($d=4096$) be zero-mean expert outputs with approximately equal second moments $\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{e}_{k_i}\|_2^2 = \sigma_e^2$ and near-mutual orthogonality $\mathbb{E}\langle \mathbf{e}_{k_i}, \mathbf{e}_{k_j}\rangle \approx 0$ for $i \neq j$, and let $\mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}(\mathbf{x})$ be orthogonal to the routed experts with $\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}\|_2^2 = \sigma_s^2$. When truncating the routed sum to $1 \le k \le K$ experts with a rescaling factor $\alpha > 0$, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{FFN}}(\alpha) = \mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}} + \alpha \sum_{i=1}^k w_i \mathbf{e}_{k_i}$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The minimum mean-squared error ($L_2$-optimal) projection onto $\text{span}(\mathbf{e}_{k_1}, \dots, \mathbf{e}_{k_k})$ is achieved at $\alpha_{\text{proj}} = 1$, whereas preserving the exact expected squared norm $\mathbb{E}\|\hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{FFN}}(\alpha)\|_2^2 = \mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{y}_{\text{FFN}}\|_2^2 = \sigma_s^2 + \sigma_e^2 \sum_{i=1}^K w_i^2$ entering the downstream \texttt{RMSNorm} requires \textbf{$L_2$-quadrature rescaling}:
    \begin{equation}
        \alpha_{L_2} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} w_i^2}}.
    \end{equation}
    \item Conversely, conventional linear $L_1$-sum rescaling $\alpha_{L_1} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^K w_i}{\sum_{i=1}^k w_i}$ strictly inflates the variance of the routed MoE branch relative to the unscaled shared expert $\mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}$ for all $1 \le k < K$ by the factor:
    \begin{equation}
        \Gamma(w) = \frac{\alpha_{L_1}^2}{\alpha_{L_2}^2} = \frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^K w_i\right)^2}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^k w_i\right)^2} \cdot \frac{\sum_{i=1}^k w_i^2}{\sum_{i=1}^K w_i^2} > 1.
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By mutual orthogonality, $\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{y}_{\text{FFN}} - \hat{\mathbf{y}}_{\text{FFN}}(\alpha)\|_2^2 = \sigma_e^2 \left[(1 - \alpha)^2 \sum_{i=1}^k w_i^2 + \sum_{i=k+1}^K w_i^2\right]$, which is minimized at $\alpha = 1$, while setting $\mathbb{E}\|\sum_{i=1}^k (\alpha w_i) \mathbf{e}_{k_i}\|_2^2 = \sigma_e^2 \alpha^2 \sum_{i=1}^k w_i^2 = \sigma_e^2 \sum_{i=1}^K w_i^2$ yields $\alpha = \alpha_{L_2}$. For typical learned-router weights at $k=4$ ($\sum_{i=1}^6 w_i = 1.50$, $\sum_{i=1}^4 w_i = 1.22$, $\sum_{i=1}^6 w_i^2 = 0.4806$, $\sum_{i=1}^4 w_i^2 = 0.4414$), linear rescaling gives $\alpha_{L_1} = 1.2295$ ($+23.0\%$ amplitude inflation, $\Gamma = 1.388$), whereas quadrature rescaling gives $\alpha_{L_2} = \sqrt{0.4806/0.4414} = 1.0435$ ($+4.35\%$).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Positive Directional Correlation $\rho$ and Downstream \texttt{RMSNorm} SNR]
If co-selected experts share a small positive pairwise directional correlation $\rho = \mathbb{E}\langle \mathbf{e}_{k_i}, \mathbf{e}_{k_j}\rangle / \sigma_e^2 \in [0, 1)$ (or a common non-zero mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}$), the exact variance-preserving factor generalizes to
\[
\alpha_{\text{gen}}(\rho) = \sqrt{\frac{(1-\rho)\sum_{i=1}^K w_i^2 + \rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^K w_i\right)^2}{(1-\rho)\sum_{i=1}^k w_i^2 + \rho\left(\sum_{i=1}^k w_i\right)^2}},
\]
which smoothly interpolates between $\alpha_{L_2}$ ($\rho \to 0$) and $\alpha_{L_1}$ (the degenerate collinear limit $\rho \to 1$). Moreover, although $\alpha_{\text{proj}}=1$ minimizes pre-normalization Euclidean MSE, the FFN output is immediately normalized by $\text{RMSNorm}(\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{y}_{\text{FFN}})$. Leaving $\alpha=1$ reduces the variance of the routed MoE branch relative to the residual stream $\mathbf{r}$ and shared expert $\mathbf{e}_{\text{shared}}$, whereas $\alpha_{L_2}$ preserves the exact signal-to-noise energy ratio between the routed and shared branches prior to \texttt{RMSNorm} division.
\end{remark}

\section{Draftless Speculative Decoding: \texttt{RouteCrack + Stockfish-PVS v2}}
\label{sec:spec}

\subsection{The CPU-MoE Speculative Golden Equation}
In standard speculative decoding \cite{leviathan2023fast}, $D$ draft tokens are verified in a single batched target forward pass. On a GPU where $D \le 8$ lies inside the compute-bound roof, verification time equals a single-token step ($c_{\text{gpu}} \approx 0$). On a memory-bandwidth-bound CPU hosting a Sparse MoE, each additional token in a verification batch activates additional routed experts from DDR5 ($\sim 0.30\times$ single-token latency per drafted token on \texttt{Nemotron-3.5-30B-A3B}), and any rejected draft token triggers an 18.4~MB Mamba-2 recurrent state rollback (\texttt{slot.spec\_ckpt}). Consequently, net wall-clock gain over $A$ accepted tokens and $W = D - A$ wasted tokens obeys:
\begin{equation}
    \text{WallGain}_{\text{CPU}} = A - 0.30\, D = 0.70\, A - 0.30\, W.
\end{equation}
A 1B neural draft model reading 600~MB per step incurs $\sim 40$~ms of DDR5 latency for $D=5$ and flushes the 64~MB L3 cache, yielding ~11.5~tok/s (slower than non-speculative decoding).

\subsection{\texttt{StockfishDrafter}: 192~KB L2 Zobrist TT \& Principal Variation Search}
To achieve high recall without DRAM traffic, \texttt{RouteCrack} (\texttt{rc\_predictor.h}) pairs a static 8-level FNV-64 hash cascade, an online session trie (\texttt{rc\_trie\_v2.h}), and an 11,382-entry multi-token macro codebook with \textbf{\texttt{StockfishDrafter} (\texttt{rc\_stockfish.h})} \cite{stockfish}. \texttt{StockfishDrafter} maintains three lockless 4-way bucketed Zobrist transposition tables (\texttt{tt5}, \texttt{tt4}, \texttt{tt3} $= 3 \times 64\text{ KB} = 192\text{ KB}$, $<19\%$ of Core~0's 1~MB L2 cache on Zen~5) with 32-bit verification keys, verified \texttt{best\_move}, explicit \texttt{refuted\_move} tracking, and history gravity ($\in [-512, +512]$).

Crucially, because static cascade tables and live-session Zobrist transposition tables have \textit{uncorrelated error modes}, gating uncertain candidates on their orthogonal agreement unlocks four previously unusable high-recall predictors: (1) Online Session Trie Rescue (92.19\% marginal precision), (2) Top-4 Cascade Disambiguation, (3) Multi-Token Macro Codebook chains (96.44\% precision), and (4) \textbf{Singular Horizon Extension up to $M=7$ tokens/step} (98.74\% marginal precision at $D=5..7$). As shown in Table~\ref{tab:synergy} (evaluated across 77,456 held-out tokens in \texttt{test\_synergy.cpp}), \texttt{Stockfish-PVS v2} adds \textbf{$+223$ accepted tokens with zero net additional wasted tokens ($W=892 \to 892$, 100.0\% net marginal precision)} and eliminates 32 Mamba-2 rollbacks.

\section{Microarchitectural \& Multi-Platform Systems Co-Design}
\label{sec:sys}

\subsection{Kernel \& Graph Optimizations in \texttt{ggml-cpu}}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Zero-Rebuild Dynamic \texttt{CSA/LID} Skip (\texttt{ggml\_dsv4\_skip\_csa\_comp})}: In \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash}, \texttt{CSA/LID} compressor blocks execute only once every 8 tokens during decode. Instead of rebuilding the 4,551-node compute graph when transitioning between compressor and non-compressor steps, we build the superset graph once, tag dummy compressor nodes with \texttt{flags |= 0x100}, and skip them in $O(1)$ inside \texttt{ggml\_graph\_compute\_thread}, eliminating 26.8~ms/tok of graph allocation overhead.
    \item \textbf{8-Slot Thread-Local \texttt{Q8\_K}/\texttt{Q8\_0} Activation Cache \& \texttt{MUL\_MAT\_ID} Row-Group Reuse}: When multiple projections share the same FP32 input tensor (e.g., \texttt{wq\_a} + \texttt{wkv\_a}, or \texttt{up\_exps} + \texttt{gate\_exps} + \texttt{shexp}), \texttt{s\_q8\_cache\_buf} quantizes the activation once and reuses it across subsequent contractions, while \texttt{g\_tls\_mmid\_ids\_valid} reuses the bucketed expert row mapping between \texttt{up\_exps} and \texttt{gate\_exps}.
    \item \textbf{In-Register 9-Stage AVX2 Fast Walsh-Hadamard Transform \& Vectorized \texttt{HC\_PRE/POST}}: \texttt{DeepSeek-V4-Flash} invokes a 512-point FWHT (\texttt{GGML\_OP\_HADAMARD}) 396 times per token. We keep all 9 butterfly stages ($s = 1, 2, \dots, 256$) inside AVX2 \texttt{YMM} registers using lane permutes (\texttt{\_mm256\_permute2f128\_ps}, \texttt{\_mm256\_shuffle\_ps}) and vectorize the 4-stream Sinkhorn-20 Hyper-Connections (\texttt{HC\_PRE/POST}), cutting non-matrix operator time in half ($19.60 \to 9.85$~ms/tok).
    \item \textbf{2~MB Transparent HugePages, 3D L1D Tiling, \& Bidirectional \texttt{\_MM\_HINT\_NTA} L3 Isolation}: Allocating weight maps and compute arenas with 2~MB-aligned \texttt{MADV\_HUGEPAGE} expands Zen~5 L2 STLB coverage to 6.14~GB (eliminating 99.6\% of page-table walks). Streaming cold MoE weights and KV tokens older than \texttt{LLAMA\_KV\_L3\_WINDOW} (2,048 on Nemotron; 1,024 on DeepSeek MLA with 8-step sparse lookahead prefetching) via \texttt{\_mm\_prefetch(..., \_MM\_HINT\_NTA)} prevents L3 victim-cache pollution.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Platform Thread-Safe Runtime Control (\texttt{Linux/macOS} \texttt{mmap} \& \texttt{Windows \_WIN32})}: Runtime MoE parameters ($k_{\text{early}}, k_{\text{mid}}, w_{\min}, \text{scale\_mode}$) are initialized on thread~0 with release-acquire atomic synchronization (\texttt{atomic\_int s\_moe\_topk}, \texttt{s\_shared\_moe\_skip\_mask}) and read from a 32-byte shared file mapping (\texttt{/tmp/dsv4\_moe\_cfg.bin} with \texttt{O\_NOFOLLOW} and \texttt{DSV4\_MOE\_CFG\_PATH} override via POSIX \texttt{mmap}, and \texttt{\%TEMP\%\textbackslash dsv4\_moe\_cfg.bin} via Win32 \texttt{CreateFileMappingA}/\texttt{MapViewOfFile}), enabling sub-nanosecond ($0.3$~ns) L1-cached inspection with zero syscalls per token while resetting the skip mask across batched prefill passes ($N > 1$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Chiplet Scaling \& Hybrid GPU+CPU Tensor-Role Splitting}
Table~\ref{tab:platforms} summarizes cross-platform scaling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{8-CCD Threadripper PRO 7985WX ($4\text{C/CCD}$ Law)}: Launching 64 threads (8C/CCD) across 8 CCDs saturates the Infinity Fabric with atomic work-stealing traffic, dropping decode speed from 80.14~tok/s to 51.80~tok/s. Selecting 4 physical cores per CCD (\texttt{-t 32}) via sysfs L3 topology discovery yields \textbf{386.00~tok/s prefill}, \textbf{80.14~tok/s cold decode}, and \textbf{111.62~tok/s warm decode}.
    \item \textbf{Hybrid GPU+CPU Tensor-Role Splitting (\texttt{-ngl 99 --n-cpu-moe $N$ --load-mode none})}: On 11--16~GB GPUs (Linux Vulkan or Windows CUDA), offloading by tensor role keeps 100\% of the dense backbone, KV cache, and Mamba-2 \texttt{SSM\_SCAN} in high-bandwidth VRAM while placing $N$ sparse MoE layers in System RAM. On an AMD RX 9070 XT 16GB, \texttt{Fractal-MXFP4} (15.2~GiB) achieves \textbf{75.1~tok/s (new code) / 80.8~tok/s (in-context refactor)} at 256K context, outperforming \texttt{Q4\_0} (69.7 / 76.2~tok/s at 16.1~GiB) while saving 0.9~GB of VRAM. On Windows 10/11 (\texttt{windows/modchip\_win.ps1}), live free VRAM is queried via \texttt{nvidia-smi} with a 550~MB WDDM safety reserve to prevent PCIe GTT paging.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations \& Negative Results}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{CPU-Only Scope of \texttt{Fractal-64} (\texttt{Q2\_0})}: Because \texttt{Fractal-64} repurposes the \texttt{Q2\_0} type ID with a custom 30-byte Block-64 layout supported in \texttt{ggml-cpu}, offloading \texttt{Q2\_0} MoE tensors to Vulkan/CUDA GPUs without a matching GPU shader produces invalid outputs; hybrid GPU+CPU setups should use \texttt{Fractal-MXFP4} or \texttt{Q4\_0}, which have native Vulkan and CUDA shaders.
    \item \textbf{Non-Temporal Prefetching on 50-Byte Unaligned \texttt{iq1\_s} Blocks}: Issuing \texttt{\_MM\_HINT\_NTA} inside \texttt{ggml\_vec\_dot\_iq1\_s\_q8\_K} degraded \texttt{iq1\_s} throughput by $\sim 5\%$ because \texttt{sizeof(block\_iq1\_s)} $= 50$~bytes ($<64$-byte cache line), causing premature eviction of cache lines straddling adjacent blocks.
    \item \textbf{Catastrophic Cliff Below Top-4 Routing}: While $L_2$-quadrature pruning from Top-6 to Top-5 ($+0.42\%$ PPL) or Top-4 ($+2.85\%$ PPL) preserves generation quality, truncating to Top-2 alters the covariance spectrum across 43--52 layers and causes EOS suppression.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
By co-designing hierarchical non-linear quantization (\texttt{Fractal-64} and analytical \texttt{SwiGLU-H2}), AVX-512/AVX2 kernels, L2/L3-resident chess-engine speculative search (\texttt{Stockfish-PVS v2}), and $L_2$-quadrature MoE / low-rank mathematics, \texttt{llama-modchip} enables frontier 30B--284B Sparse MoE inference at 256K context on commodity 64~GB CPUs and consumer GPU+CPU hybrids. Full source code, C/C++ patches, and reproducible benchmarks are available at \url{https://github.com/synchopate/llama-modchip}.
